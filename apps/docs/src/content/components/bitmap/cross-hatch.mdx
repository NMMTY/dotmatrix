The same per-cell luminance averaging as [Halftone](/components/bitmap/halftone), mapped to a
*count* of overlaid hatch directions instead of a dot radius. Light cells get no strokes at all,
progressively darker cells add one direction at a time — `\`, then `/`, then `—`, then finally `|`
for the darkest cells — the same layering classic engravings and pen illustrations use to build up
shadow. Ink color is read from the canvas's own computed `color` at draw time, so it follows the
current theme.

## Compared to Dither and Halftone

All three bitmap components sample an image and reduce it to this system's monochrome language,
but they read very differently at the same source image:

- **[Dither](/components/bitmap/dither)** is per-pixel and textural — good for photographs where
  you want the *whole* image visibly built from noise.
- **[Halftone](/components/bitmap/halftone)** is per-cell and even — a uniform screen that reads
  as tone from a distance, closest to a printed photograph.
- **CrossHatch** is per-cell and directional — the strokes themselves are visible up close, giving
  a more illustrative, hand-drawn feel than either of the other two.
- **[AsciiArt](/components/bitmap/ascii-art)** is per-cell and typographic — density expressed as
  characters rather than dots or strokes, the most literal "bitmap" of the four.

Reach for `CrossHatch` when the image is more decorative than informational — a hero illustration
or an avatar — where the hatching itself is part of the appeal rather than a means to preserve
detail.

`src` needs to be same-origin or CORS-enabled for the effect to actually apply — see [Dither's
notes on loading external images](/components/bitmap/dither#loading-external-images).
